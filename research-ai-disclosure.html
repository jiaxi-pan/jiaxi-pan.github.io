Creator AI-Use Disclosure on Xiaohongshu and Instagram

Method
Close reading of posts and comments
Keywords
AI disclosure, authenticity, brand design, Xiaohongshu, Instagram

While generative AI can now produce professional brand design, its role in real-world practice is complex. This project asks how designers on Xiaohongshu and Instagram disclose or frame their use of AI, signal authenticity, and how audiences respond. I collected nineteen brand design posts, ten on Xiaohongshu and nine on Instagram, that are fully AI-generated, AI-assisted, or human-designed with AI-generated presentation, and examined their titles, captions, platform labels, and comments. As a bilingual designer who has observed these practices from within both cultural contexts, I can read and interpret each side in its own language.

Early findings

  1. Disclosure follows the role AI played. All sixteen posts where AI made or assisted the design named AI in their own title, caption or tag. On Xiaohongshu, three posts used AI only for mockups or images. Two of them said nothing, and AI use came out only when a commenter asked.
  2. Platform labels miss many posts. An AI label appeared on five of ten Xiaohongshu posts and five of nine Instagram posts. Neither system seems able to detect all AI-generated content on its own.
  3. Prompt requests cluster in a few posts. Three posts hold nearly all of about 2,660 requests: 624 and 58 on Xiaohongshu, and about 1,964 on Instagram. So Instagram audiences do ask for prompts under some posts, which complicates what I saw in my illustration study.
  4. Once audiences know AI was used, they ask how far it went. One commenter asked, “Did AI come up with this color scheme itself, too?”
  5. In my reading, brand designs on Instagram are more minimal in each image, while creators on Xiaohongshu show more products.
