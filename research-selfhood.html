Negotiating Selfhood with AI: Chinese International Students' Experiences of Cultural Values, Language, and Self-Reflection

Author
Jiaxi (Peggy) Pan, Boston University
Supervisor
Seth Looper, Lo/Be Lab
Data
43 survey responses and 10 semi-structured interviews
Method
Bilingual survey and semi-structured interviews, reflexive thematic analysis
Keywords
international students; generative AI; self-reflection; cultural self-construal; relational credit

Abstract

Chinese international students in U.S. higher education increasingly use conversational AI, including Western tools such as ChatGPT and Chinese tools such as Doubao, to reflect on their goals and sense of self. Drawing on the looking-glass self, self-construal theory and the Conservation of Resources model, this exploratory study combines a survey of 43 international students, 37 of them Chinese, with ten semi-structured interviews. Thirty-two of the 37 Chinese respondents reported using AI this way, most often for career and academic decisions. Students valued AI as a confidant that asks nothing back, which I describe with the tentative concept of relational credit. Some perceived AI as putting the individual first or as overly affirming, though these perceptions varied across tools. Students kept authority over the result, choosing languages and tools for different problems and rewriting or rejecting what AI offered. AI-mediated reflection appears less as cultural absorption than as a negotiated practice.

Ethics and consent

Participation was voluntary. Participants were recruited through the author's personal networks on WeChat and Instagram, and some passed the survey on to friends. The survey opened with a consent question that every respondent accepted; interviewees gave verbal consent after a short introduction to the study. The survey did not ask for names, interviews were recorded with permission, and participants are identified by number. Recordings were deleted after transcription; only de-identified notes and quotes were kept. One participant was a client of the supervisor's consulting practice. Interviews were transcribed automatically on the recording device and corrected by hand. ChatGPT, with model training turned off, was used to help translate the Mandarin interviews and check descriptive figures; every quoted passage was verified against the transcripts. The study was exploratory and was not reviewed by an institutional review board.
