Reading

What I am reading on AI, creators and platforms, with a note on each.

Creator culture in China

Craig, D., Lin, J., & Cunningham, S. (2021). Wanghong as social media entertainment in China. Palgrave Macmillan. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-65376-7

This book describes the wanghong industry as a social media ecology grown from China's own political, cultural, and platform conditions, and different from Western systems. It gives my study its main frame: reading audience engagement inside this ecology, and watching how the ecology shifts as AI arrives.

AI, creators and audiences

Zhang, Q., & Negus, K. (2026). Introduction to artificial intelligence in the creator economy: Creativities, adaptation, agency, and trust. Global Media and China, 11(3), 291-302. https://doi.org/10.1177/20594364261466625

Zhang and Negus frame AI in the creator economy through four themes: creativities, adaptation, agency and trust. They describe new opportunities alongside uncertainty about authorship and artistic value, which gives my platform comparison a wider frame.

Wang, C., & Kim, S.-H. (2026). What is the future of AI-generated art? An AI-assisted netnography of resistance on Chinese social media platforms. Futures, 180, Article 103815. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.futures.2026.103815

Wang and Kim trace why some users resist AI-generated art, naming concerns such as emotional absence and copyright, while also finding that acceptance grows as the technology matures.

Park, J., Oh, C., & Kim, H. Y. (2024). AI vs. human-generated content and accounts on Instagram: User preferences, evaluations, and ethical considerations. Technology in Society, 79, Article 102705. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techsoc.2024.102705

In a controlled user study, people evaluated AI accounts and their posts about as favorably as those of human influencers, and often could not tell them apart.

Influencers and platforms

Cunningham, S., & Craig, D. (2019). Social media entertainment: The new intersection of Hollywood and Silicon Valley. NYU Press.

Social media is shifting the power of entertainment production from traditional Hollywood institutions to platforms and creators.

Beichert, M., Bayerl, A., Goldenberg, J., & Lanz, A. (2024). Revenue generation through influencer marketing. Journal of Marketing, 88(4), 40-63. https://doi.org/10.1177/00222429231217471

Nano-influencers have closer relationships and stronger engagement with their followers, resulting in higher commercial conversion rates compared to macro-influencers.

Marwick, A. E. (2015). Instafame: Luxury selfies in the attention economy. Public Culture, 27(1), 137-160. https://doi.org/10.1215/08992363-2798379

Marwick examines the “Instafamous,” a new kind of Instagram celebrity, and how it relates to celebrity and tabloid culture. It helps me read Instagram as a place for display, which fits the admiring comments I found there.
